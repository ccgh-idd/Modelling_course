Why are we here?

Mathematical models increasingly shape public health decisions: from outbreak response to vaccine introduction and resource allocation.

Understand

Make assumptions about transmission explicit and explore their consequences.

Predict

Nowcast and forecast epidemics to support real-time response.

Compare

Evaluate interventions and their cost-effectiveness before implementation.

Critique

Judge whether a model is fit to inform the decision at hand.

Who is this course for?

Epidemiologists & health professionals

Build the ability to critically review models and their use in policy.

Data scientists

Learn how to build, fit and interpret infectious disease models.

You bring different strengths: the group work is designed so that you learn from each other.

Three kinds of sessions

Lectures (1 h)Concepts, methods and examples from real public health questions.
Practicals (1.5 h)Hands-on exercises in Excel and R that directly follow each lecture, supported by tutors.
Group workApply what you learn to a policy question in small groups, culminating in presentations.

Group work: from model to recommendation

  • Group work I: form groups, choose a topic and scope your modelling question
  • Group work II: discuss readings, get peer feedback and sharpen the policy question
  • Group work III: prepare a policy-facing presentation
  • Group presentations: present to a mock NITAG and defend your assumptions
Focus on clear argument, transparent assumptions and honest communication of uncertainty.

Assessment

Group presentation

NITAG-style presentation and discussion in the final days of the course.

Multiple-choice exam

Short exam in the final session covering the key concepts from all lectures.

Successful completion earns 3 ECTS and a course certificate.
